Procedural Generation
======================

### Overview

Procedural generation is a method of creating data algorithmically, as opposed to manually, using a combination of human-generated content and algorithms coupled with computer-generated randomness and processing power.

### Definition

Procedural generation refers to the process that computes a particular function, often resulting in geometric patterns, textures, and meshes.

### Applications

*   **Computer Graphics**: Used to create textures and 3D models.
*   **Video Games**: Automatically create large amounts of content, such as levels, textures, and complete worlds.
*   **Sound**: Procedurally generated, used in speech synthesis and music composition.
*   **Tabletop Role-Playing Games**: Used to generate dungeons, terrain, and other world elements.

### History

*   Procedural generation was originally used in video games to aid in generating levels, textures, and complete worlds with little human contribution.
*   Early examples include NetHack (1980), Rogue (1980), and Beneath Apple Manor (1978).
*   Modern applications include the Elder Scrolls II: Daggerfall, Soldier of Fortune, and Just Cause.

### Concerns

*   "Procedural oatmeal" - generated content may be perceived as similar and lack uniqueness.

### APIs and Tools

*   **L-Systems**: Used to generate realistic-looking tree models.
*   **Fractals**: Geometric patterns that can be generated procedurally.
*   **Pseudorandom Number Generators (PRNGs)**: Used to generate large game worlds that appear to be premade.
